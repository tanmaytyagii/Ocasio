# Ocasio: competitor research and founder business model

Working strategy v1 • 20 November 2026

Prepared for three founders with hometown connections in Karnal and Muzaffarnagar and study/work connections in Delhi NCR. India is the assumed market. Budget, founder hours and existing vendor relationships are not yet confirmed. All Ocasio prices, budgets, scores and targets below are proposed experiments, not market facts or forecasts.

## 1. The business you are building

Ocasio helps people discover what their occasion could become, choose suitable local professionals, and book with clear expectations. Vendors provide the actual services. Ocasio owns selection, matching, booking clarity and support for the promises it makes.

Suggested category: a curated local marketplace for celebrations and event services.

The customer has three linked problems: “What is possible?”, “Who can actually do it here?” and “How do I know I will receive what I paid for?” A searchable directory answers only part of the second question. Ocasio should connect inspiration to local availability, comparable scope and a supported booking.

Core hypothesis: a meaningful group of customers will book through Ocasio, and vendors will pay a sustainable success fee, when it reduces uncertainty and delivers qualified demand. This remains to be tested with paid bookings.

### Evidence in your three markets

Delhi NCR already has a substantial celebration catalogue from CherishX. One reviewed page displayed a basic birthday decoration at ₹1,499 and more elaborate listings above ₹10,000. These are displayed listing prices, not comparable all-inclusive quotes or a citywide average. This makes a lowest-price decoration strategy unattractive for a founder-assisted business.

Karnal is already served by a dedicated WedMeGood city page. Muzaffarnagar has WedMeGood vendor listings and Justdial event-decorator listings. Neither hometown is an untouched online market. Listing presence does not establish booking activity, local satisfaction or competitive service quality.

### The opportunity to validate

The proposed opening is an accessible local service that translates a customer's occasion and budget into a few feasible ideas, obtains comparable quotes, and supports the resulting booking. Curated vendors and trustworthy reviews alone are not enough of a distinction. Ocasio must prove a better experience within a tightly defined local segment.

Defensibility could grow from actual fulfilment records, accurate local package data, responsive vendor relationships and referrals. A large directory, a logo or a generic AI planner would not establish that advantage by itself.

## 3. Geography: use all three networks, control the service footprint

Your hometown relationships may reduce the effort of finding and checking vendors. Your NCR connections provide access to potential customers and organisations. These are founder advantages to test, not proof that acquisition will be cheap.

Run discovery in all three places during the first month. Offer confirmed bookings only within explicitly published localities and categories that have checked suppliers and an available support owner.

| Market | First experiment | Admission requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Karnal | Family/referral demand and local vendor interviews | Named local responder, backup suppliers and viable written quotes |
| Muzaffarnagar | Same experiment through independent customer and supplier networks | Same standard; hometown familiarity cannot replace checks |
| One NCR cluster | Test a few adjacent neighbourhoods near founder work/study access | Exact postal-code coverage, workable travel time and assigned support |

Do not choose Gurugram, Noida or Delhi solely for prestige or assumed spending power. Choose the cluster where the team can recruit suitable vendors and handle problems promptly. Suppliers should normally fulfil locally; cross-city travel must be quoted explicitly.

Suggested 30-day discovery sample: 10 potential customers and five vendors per market, including people outside friends and family. This is directional qualitative research, not a representative survey.

Score each market on founder/support availability (25%), supplier readiness and backups (25%), qualified demand and willingness to book (25%), expected contribution after acquisition (15%), and travel burden (10%). Use results to choose the primary pilot; keep the other two enquiry-led until ready. If all three independently meet the standards, a capped pilot in all three is reasonable.

## 4. First customer and service offer

Initial customer hypothesis: a working adult or family organising a birthday, anniversary, baby shower, roka or small celebration, with a real date and location, who wants help choosing and booking services. A person who knows the occasion but not which suppliers to choose fits your insight particularly well.

Test a ₹15,000–₹75,000 services basket, excluding venue unless explicitly included. This is a proposed target range, not evidence of average local spending. Allow smaller simple orders when they can be handled efficiently; avoid giving each a long bespoke consultation.

Start with decor, photography and an optional host/performer. These provide a visible result and can be specified relatively clearly. Begin with single-service bookings, then offer two-service combinations once handoffs work. Venues, catering and large weddings can follow when supplier capacity and operating processes support them. Do not impose many interdependent services just to raise order value.

Create 8–12 locally feasible idea cards. Each should show a real or clearly labelled concept image, suitable occasion, guest-count assumptions, space requirements, inclusions/exclusions, indicative price range, customisation options and availability-check status. Never present a concept image as a completed customer event.

The promise is “help me choose confidently,” not an unlimited free planning service. A first consultation can be capped at 15–20 minutes with two or three suitable choices.

## 5. Business model

| Element | Proposed design |
|---|---|
| Customer value | Relevant ideas, checked local suppliers, comparable quotes and booking support |
| Vendor value | Qualified briefs, fewer unproductive enquiries, clear scope, timely settlement and evidence-based reputation |
| Distribution | Founder referrals, customer referrals, local content, venue/cafe/community partnerships; small paid tests later |
| Key work | Vetting, catalogue accuracy, matching, quote comparison, confirmation, support and review collection |
| Resources | Local vendor network, one booking record, support coverage, real work portfolio and founder time |
| Revenue | Negotiated vendor success fees on completed eligible bookings; optional separately priced customer assistance later |
| Costs | Acquisition, supplier checks, support labour, payment handling, tools, travel, refunds/remediation and overhead |

### Revenue recommendation

Start with free browsing and free basic enquiry. Negotiate a category-specific vendor success fee; test 10–15% as a hypothesis, not as a competitor benchmark or a guaranteed achievable rate. Larger venues and low-margin services may need a lower percentage or fixed success fee. Vendor willingness and contribution decide the eventual rate.

Use a written agreement defining the commission base, attribution, cancellation treatment and due date. Recognise the operating success metric on completed work; deposits are not automatically earned platform revenue.

Do not depend on listing subscriptions at launch: you have not yet demonstrated enough demand to justify them. Later subscriptions could fund genuine vendor tools. If advertising is introduced, label it and keep it separate from quality checks and organic recommendations.

An optional paid shortlist/assistance service can be tested later, with an explicit scope, customer fee and explanation of any vendor commission. Avoid undisclosed markups or charging both parties without explaining the arrangement.

### How a booking works

1. Customer submits occasion, date, locality, budget, guest count, venue status and preferred style.
2. Ocasio checks feasibility and customer consent before sharing the brief with selected vendors.
3. Up to three suitable vendors confirm availability and issue itemised offers.
4. Customer compares scope, timing, extras, payment schedule and cancellation rules.
5. Vendor and customer approve a final booking summary; Ocasio records its own service and fee.
6. Deposit and remaining payment follow the agreed, documented schedule. A booking is confirmed only after the required acceptance and payment steps.
7. Ocasio checks readiness at agreed milestones and provides its published support channel.
8. Completion is recorded, disputes are tracked, fees reconciled and a booking-linked review requested.

For a manual pilot, payment directly to the vendor with receipts and a tracked commission invoice can reduce setup complexity, but commission collection and leakage become risks. If using platform collection, first select a payment provider and confirm the permitted settlement, refund and accounting setup with qualified advisers. Do not hold pooled customer money informally or call ordinary collections “escrow.” This report does not establish the legal or tax treatment of either route.

## 6. Vendor admission and continuing quality

Application → business/identity and payment-detail checks → original portfolio check → customer references → interview/site visit or demonstration as appropriate → written commercial and fulfilment terms → trial status → monitored bookings → periodic reassessment.

Suggested admission score: demonstrated work 30 points; reference checks 20; scope/pricing clarity 15; communication 15; capacity and backup arrangements 10; documentation 10. A proposed 75/100 threshold is only an internal decision aid. Fraud, unsafe work or refusal to document commitments are disqualifiers regardless of score. Check category-specific documents where relevant with professional guidance.

Track arrival/setup punctuality, scope changes, cancellations, complaints, resolution time and customer feedback. Show review count and recency alongside rating. Distinguish “identity checked,” “portfolio checked” and “completed Ocasio bookings”; a single generic verified badge can overstate what was checked.

New vendors should have a visible new-vendor status. Publish genuine negative reviews under a consistent moderation policy; permit a vendor response and protect customer privacy. Do not sell favourable ratings or make review removal part of a paid plan.

Target two independent supplier options per supported category/locality before expanding. A listed backup is not a guaranteed replacement: availability must be reconfirmed. Never promise universal replacement, refunds or flawless outcomes without the operational ability and funding to honour them.

## 7. Support, leakage and capacity

Asset-light still requires operational work. Customers will associate vendor failures with Ocasio. Publish who handles matching, vendor delivery, event-wide coordination and complaints. A support owner is not automatically an on-site event manager.

For each booking: name a primary owner and backup, record a vendor contact and setup deadline, confirm site restrictions, and prepare a response for late arrival or failure. Offer replacement sourcing when feasible, document customer approval for changes, and apply the agreed refund/remedy terms. A one-time occasion cannot always be repaired afterwards.

Because founders study/work, do not advertise 24/7 help without staffing it. Agree enquiry hours and event-specific support windows before taking deposits. Weekend events still need weekday preparation. If none of the founders can cover an event window, decline or arrange a qualified support resource before accepting it.

Control leakage by making the booking record, clear scope, payment traceability, support and review eligibility useful. Avoid relying on hidden contact details. Track enquiries, confirmed bookings, completed bookings, cancellations and reported direct conversions; collect agreed commissions consistently.

Initial capacity experiment: no more than two simultaneous supported events per available owner, reducing the limit when travel or complexity requires it. Measure actual hours per booking before raising the cap.

## 8. Economics: an illustrative completed vendor booking

The unit here is one vendor-service booking, not a whole event or total family event spending. An event can contain multiple bookings; report both separately. Amounts below exclude tax and are assumptions. Replace them with observed data.

| Item | Illustrative amount |
|---|---:|
| Commissionable vendor service value | ₹25,000 |
| Vendor success fee at 12% | ₹3,000 |
| Payment/collection cost allocated to Ocasio | ₹500 |
| Variable support labour, including valued founder time | ₹400 |
| Remediation/refund provision | ₹250 |
| Contribution before customer acquisition | ₹1,850 |
| Acquisition cost per completed booking | ₹700 |
| Contribution after acquisition | ₹1,150 |

The ₹500 is a placeholder, not a provider quote, and depends strongly on whether Ocasio processes the whole service payment or only its fee. The vendor service cost is not deducted again in this agency-style illustration: ₹3,000 is the platform fee, not ₹25,000 of platform revenue. An accountant must determine actual reporting and tax treatment.

At ₹60,000 monthly fixed operating costs, this example requires approximately 53 completed bookings per month to cover those costs. Founder salaries are excluded from that fixed-cost assumption; if included, break-even rises. With the same costs but ₹1,500 acquisition cost, contribution falls to ₹350 and break-even rises to about 172 bookings. A three-founder side project may not have that capacity.

A ₹3,000 service at 12% produces only ₹360 in fee revenue before any cost. Such orders need efficient self-service or low acquisition/support costs. This is why cheap decor plus unlimited manual assistance is a weak starting model.

Measure acquisition cost using completed paying bookings, not enquiries. Allocate failed-lead handling, cancellations, discounts and founder time as well. Do not assume frequent customer repeat purchases; test referrals and cross-occasion use before using them in lifetime-value projections.

## 9. Ninety-day launch

Days 1–15: agree weekly founder hours and roles; interview 30 customers and 15 vendors across the three markets; document recent real event purchases, problems, alternatives, quote differences and vendor willingness to pay. Audit 10 competing offers per market where available, using the same fictional brief only for internal comparison and clearly identifying research if contacting providers. Do not pretend public starting prices are comparable final quotes.

Days 16–30: select the primary local service area from evidence; approve 8–12 suppliers across the initial categories; obtain backup coverage; publish 8–12 feasible idea cards; prepare booking summaries, support process and commercial terms. Test the whole booking and cancellation flow internally. Open only the localities that meet readiness standards.

Days 31–60: run a capped pilot targeting 10–15 paid events, reporting vendor bookings separately. Include customers outside the founders' personal circle. Capture actual scope, final price, support time, acquisition source and fulfilment outcomes. Ask permission for photos/testimonials. Record discounts so subsidised results are visible.

Days 61–90: aim for 20–30 cumulative paid events if capacity allows. Concentrate on the market/category with the best evidence. Test a small paid acquisition channel only after successful fulfilment. Fix recurring failures before adding geography or categories.

Suggested expansion gates: positive contribution after realistic acquisition and support costs; at least 20 completed events with no unresolved serious incidents; approximately 95% or better punctual setup over a sufficiently documented sample; dependable backup coverage; and a support rota that works without founders repeatedly missing work. These are proposed operating gates, not statistically established benchmarks. Always show numerator/denominator for small samples.

Pause a locality or category when supplier cancellations recur, support cannot cover the accepted events, or contribution remains negative without a credible improvement. Do not use booking volume alone as permission to expand.

## 10. Three-founder responsibilities

Assign based on availability and skill rather than hometown alone. One founder owns supplier admission, rate cards and capacity; one owns demand, customer briefs, conversion and partnerships; one owns product, booking records, finance reporting and support operations. Every event still needs one named decision-maker and a backup.

Agree equity, vesting, weekly commitment, expense approvals, decision rights and an exit/deadlock process in writing with professional help. Family/friend vendors should pass the same checks and disclose relationships internally. Maintain one shared pipeline and weekly cash/contribution review.

## 11. Product and brand implications

The current repository is a starting implementation, not evidence that the service is launch ready. Before accepting public bookings, inspect and verify the real customer/vendor journey, database setup, payments, permissions and deployment. This report is not a technical audit.

Priority product experience: occasion-first enquiry, exact service area, locally feasible ideas, accurate vendor evidence, itemised offers, written confirmation, cancellation visibility, support contact and booking-linked reviews. Manual matching is acceptable in the pilot. Do not imply instant availability when a person must check it.

Brand foundation: three friends want people to celebrate confidently even when they do not have industry contacts or know the available possibilities. Build the full story from genuine founder experience; do not invent a dramatic origin event or years of expertise.

Working positioning: “Ocasio helps you discover and book the right local people for your occasion—with ideas that fit, clear choices and support along the way.”

Working tagline territory: “Make room for the moment.” This is a creative direction, not a cleared or final trademark. The brand should feel warm, resourceful, contemporary and dependable. Promise specific checks and behaviours rather than “best,” “cheapest” or guaranteed perfection.

After pilot decisions: finalise audience and positioning, founder story, promise, voice, naming/availability checks, visual direction, logo system, colour/type system, photography, supplier badges, proposal templates and social/website applications. A complete brand kit should express the operating model rather than cover an unresolved one.

## 12. Decisions and unanswered research

Confirm available cash, founder hours and support coverage; exact NCR cluster; actual trusted vendor contacts; customer segment with willingness to book; category-specific vendor fees; quote comparability; payment/refund setup; seasonality; and local acquisition economics. No reliable city-level market sizing or competitor conversion/profit data was established in this research, so none is asserted.

The next decisive evidence is paid, successfully fulfilled local bookings with honest cost records. Use the three geographies as a learning advantage, and earn the right to expand service coverage through measurable delivery.
