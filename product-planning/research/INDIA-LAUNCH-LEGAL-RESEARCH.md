# India launch legal research · Ocasio

Checked 23 November 2026. Status: researched requirements and implementation proposals, not approved legal policies or certification. Three research workstreams covered privacy/security, consumer/vendor duties, and payments/tax. Sources below are official government/regulator publications. Counsel and a chartered accountant must resolve applicability to the actual entity, contracts, categories and money flow before launch. No provider onboarding, company incorporation, trademark clearance or professional sign-off was performed.

Clarification: the founder meant CSRF (cross-site request forgery) protection. It is an application-security requirement, not a separate legal certification. DPDP and the other launch-law research remain independently in scope.

## 1. Latest confirmed business directions

- Vendor acceptance confirms a customer booking; no deposit is required to confirm.
- Subsequent payment timing follows each vendor's disclosed terms, not an undisclosed new confirmation condition.
- Customer chooses direct-vendor or through-Ocasio payment; the latter requires a feasible approved arrangement.
- Multi-vendor packages retain accepted components if another vendor declines; customer selects the replacement.
- Vendor commission, one-time and recurring vendor charges, and optional paid personalised assistance are intended. Vendor charges are payable after approval; amounts/intervals and whether both charge types apply to each vendor remain open.
- Customer onboarding is free. Personalised assistance is paid and includes sourcing, booking, follow-ups, coordination and event-day assistance; on-site support depends on availability.
- Ocasio wants standard platform cancellation rules. No time bands or percentages have been approved.

## 2. Current versus future law

| Instrument | Status on research date | Launch implication |
|---|---|---|
| Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules 2020, with notified 2021 amendment/corrigendum | Existing framework | Marketplace/service, seller and grievance applicability review now |
| E-Commerce Amendment Rules 2026 | Notified; effective **1 January 2027** | Build for imminent changes if launching/operating then |
| DPDP Act/Rules | Final framework, phased commencement | Institutional/Consent Manager phases commenced; principal business duties in future eighteen-month phase |
| Dark Patterns Guidelines 2023 | Already notified and operative | Deceptive design must not wait for 2027 audit changes |
| RBI PA directions and 2026 e-mandate framework | Current instruments located | Select payment architecture and recurring billing using current sources |
| IT/SPDI and CERT-In | Separate current applicability assessment | DPDP transition is not a privacy/security exemption |

No universal industry cancellation tariff was established in this research. Business practice, an industry standard, a regulator rule and a statutory obligation are different kinds of authority.

## 3. Consumer marketplace duties

The [2020 Gazette, Rules 2–6](https://consumeraffairs.gov.in/public/upload/files/E%20commerce%20rules_1732703966.pdf) covers services and marketplace models. It requires platform/seller disclosures, affirmative purchase consent, complaint tracking, public ranking information and seller-description accuracy. Rule 4 requires grievance acknowledgement within 48 hours and redress within one month, limits one-sided cancellation charging, and addresses accepted refunds within reasonable/applicable periods. Rule 6 prohibits seller impersonation in reviews and addresses deficient, nonconforming or late service remedies, with a specified force-majeure exception for delay. Vendor policies cannot simply displace these protections.

The [2021 notified amendment](https://consumeraffairs.gov.in/public/upload/files/Consumer%20Protection%20%28E-Commerce%29%20%28Amendment%29%20Rules%2C%202021_1732704241.pdf) replaces Rule 4(1) and requires the described entity categories to appoint an India-resident nodal officer/alternate senior designated functionary. Entity applicability must be checked. The separate June 2021 consultation is not a substitute for notified law.

The [CCPA seller-disclosure advisory](https://ccpa.doca.gov.in/files/Advisory-2.pdf) emphasises displaying seller-provided information, including grievance contacts. Public profiles and accepted offers therefore need a legal disclosure view, not merely a brand gallery or Ocasio support email. Privacy/anti-leakage preferences must be reconciled with mandatory disclosures.

### 2027 readiness

The [2026 Gazette amendment](https://consumeraffairs.gov.in/public/upload/files/E%20Commerce%20Amendment%20Rules%202026_1789128659.pdf) adds complaint copies, mandatory NCH convergence, sponsored-listing labels, prior-price information, seller attribution on invoices, annual dark-pattern audit/certificate, ranking disclosure changes, and specific data-use/bundled-fee restrictions. It commences 1 January 2027; it is not effective on the research date. The common-brand/associated-seller data restrictions are purpose-specific, not a new blanket description of all data use. The bundled-fee clause is not a general ban on multi-vendor event packages. Exact application to Ocasio's branding and paid assistance requires review.

The [official September 2026 announcement](https://www.pib.gov.in/newsite/erelcontent.aspx?lang=2&reg=48&relid=294532) confirms commencement and describes prior price as the lowest offered price in the preceding 30 days. A future discount UI needs retained offer history; fabricated strike-through prices are not acceptable evidence.

### Dark patterns and review evidence

The [notified 2023 guidelines](https://ccpa.doca.gov.in/files/The%20Guidelines%20for%20Prevention%20and%20Regulation%20of%20Dark%20Patterns,%202023_1732707717.pdf) address deceptive interfaces such as false urgency, basket sneaking, drip pricing and subscription traps. Proposed checks: no fake scarcity; no pre-added premium assistance; upfront price breakdown; equally understandable cancellation/renewal controls; marketing choice separate from booking agreement. A website badge alone is not an audit.

[BIS's February 2026 review-process scheme](https://www.bis.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/scheme-online-review-Feb-26.pdf) concerns certification of review administration processes, not proof every review is true. A universal mandatory certification requirement was not established; recheck any later QCO before launch. Booking evidence, consistent moderation, source labels, response/appeal history and privacy redaction are proposed controls, not a claimed certification.

## 4. Cancellation: policy design, not invented percentages

The [Contract Act, sections 73–74](https://www.wbconsumers.gov.in/writereaddata/ACT%20%26%20RULES/Relevant%20Act%20%26%20Rules/the-indian-contract-act-1872.pdf) requires consideration of compensation principles; naming a penalty does not automatically establish entitlement to collect it. Counsel should assess enforceability, reasonable compensation and consumer-rule interaction. This report does not decide individual disputes.

Proposed standard-policy structure for review:

| Scenario | Required policy decision |
|---|---|
| Request withdrawn before supplier acceptance | Whether any specifically authorised separate assistance work remains payable; never invent a confirmed-service cancellation |
| Customer cancels confirmed, unpaid service | Any charge must be expressly agreed and legally reviewed; absence of deposit does not settle liability either way |
| Customer cancels paid service | Refund eligibility, documented committed work/costs, time band and execution route |
| Vendor cancels/no-shows | Customer remedy, replacement consent, cost allocation and vendor accountability |
| Deficient/changed/late performance | Preserve applicable statutory remedies rather than applying a blanket voluntary-cancellation charge |
| Reschedule/weather/site restrictions | Fresh capacity, approved change and treatment of incurred costs; no generic force-majeure assumption |
| One package component fails | Affect only that component under confirmed product direction; review dependencies and replacement price explicitly |
| Paid assistance cancelled | Separate delivered assistance from unperformed work and unconfirmed physical attendance |

Exact windows/amounts, charge symmetry, refund funding and legal wording remain unresolved. Do not publish competitor percentages as “industry law,” impose a surprise debit, or promise every refund in an invented fixed number of days.

## 5. DPDP: precise commencement

[G.S.R. 843(E)](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/c56ceae6c383460ca69577428d36828b.pdf) stages the Act: initial provisions on publication; section 6(9)/27(1)(d) after one year; principal sections 3–17 and other listed provisions after eighteen months. Rules 1, 2 and 17–21 commence on publication; Rule 4 after one year; remaining listed rules after eighteen months. Thus substantive business duties are not generally commenced on 23 November 2026. Operating a consent preference screen does not automatically make Ocasio a statutory Consent Manager.

Publication-date precision: printed November 2025 mastheads say 13 November, electronic Gazette codes/PIB refer to 14 November. Using the printed masthead gives 13 November 2026/13 May 2027; have counsel confirm operative date rather than relying on the one-day difference. Status today is the same either way. [December corrigenda](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/12/3c7ebbae0e5456f493f486e6845df86b.pdf) correct wording without changing elapsed periods. [October 2026 order](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/10/daf495a7b5798a3dfa72a944b51fc176.pdf) makes textual corrections to sections 9/10, not a new general commencement date.

### Future duties to design for

[Act sections 2–16](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf) address processing scope, lawful grounds, notice/consent, accountability, processors, safeguards, erasure/retention, grievance handling and individual rights. Determine Ocasio/vendor/provider roles by actual purpose and means, not a blanket label. Children are under eighteen; parental/guardian consent and restrictions require assessment even for birthday media uploaded by an adult. Significant Data Fiduciary obligations depend on designation; do not presume that status. DPDP does not itself establish an unconditional India-only hosting mandate for every dataset.

[Final Rules 3, 6–15](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf) detail independent notices, safeguards, parental verification, rights mechanisms and breach reporting. Rule 7 calls for initial Board/affected-person notice without delay and detailed Board information within 72 hours, subject to extension—not a universal 72-hour delay allowance. Rule 8(3) specifies a one-year minimum for covered data/log retention purposes. The Third Schedule's inactivity period is threshold-specific, not a blanket startup rule. Reconcile retention, legal holds and erasure in a reviewed schedule. These substantive rules remain in the future phase.

### Present privacy/security assessment

The [SPDI Rules 2011](https://indiacode.gov.in/server/api/core/bitstreams/e9545daf-b012-49cd-a697-388039b9b7e0/content) require a current entity/data-specific review of privacy, sensitive-data handling, security and grievance obligations. DPDP's omission of IT Act section 43A is deferred. Passwords, financial and health information require particular attention; do not categorise every ordinary photograph/address as SPDI automatically.

[CERT-In's 2022 directions](https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/CERT-In_Directions_70B_28.04.2022.pdf) separately address covered-entity incident reporting, contact designation, time synchronisation and 180-day ICT log retention within India. Listed reportable incidents can trigger a six-hour clock. This is distinct from DPDP's future detailed Board report. Prepare incident classification and reporting ownership before launch; do not wait for a complete forensic report to assess reporting duties.

## 6. Payments and recurring vendor billing

The [RBI PA Master Direction, updated 11 November 2026](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=12896) regulates payment aggregation based on actual activity, including merchant onboarding, settlement and escrow controls. Neither “every marketplace needs its own PA licence” nor “a gateway makes any collection arrangement permissible” is established. Obtain written provider/counsel review of the exact marketplace collection/refund/settlement flow. Do not route customer funds through an ordinary Ocasio account and assume distribution is merely a technical feature. Refund execution and chargeback rights need separate records; normal PA refunds go to the original method unless the payer instructs another eligible own method.

The [RBI E-mandate Framework, 21 April 2026](https://rbi.org.in/scripts/FS_Notification.aspx?Id=13374&Mode=0&fn=9) consolidates recurring-payment rules, including authenticated registration/first transaction, mandate withdrawal and generally at least 24-hour pre-debit notice. Vendor approval is not mandate consent. Recurring invoices may be manually paid; recurring does not automatically mean autopay. If autopay is selected, use provider-managed mandate references and handle revocation, expiry, failed renewal and cancellation. Do not store card credentials or silently initiate renewals. One-time and recurring charge schedules need separate acceptance and invoices.

## 7. Tax, categories and communications

[CGST section 52](https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content-page/explore-act/1000325/1000001) links relevant TCS to supplies where consideration is collected by the operator; separate section 9(5) rules may also matter. Direct payment does not establish zero marketplace tax obligations. CA review must cover commission, vendor charges, assistance, registration, place of supply, supplier status, credit notes, returns and actual money flow. Do not treat settlement net of commission as automatically resolving collection classification. Historical blanket 1% TCS figures are stale; [Notification 15/2024](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/node/5015) amended the rate. Reconfirm applicable central/state/IGST rates at launch rather than hardcoding this report.

[CBIC Circular 167/23/2021](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/pdf/Circular-167-17-12-2021-GST.pdf) illustrates special restaurant-service treatment through operators. A caterer is not automatically a restaurant, an event hall is not automatically accommodation, and transport offerings require their own classification.

If food services enter scope, [FSSAI licensing FAQs, Q78–80](https://fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/FAQs_Licensing_Registration_26_07_2022.pdf) distinguish listing-only platforms from transactional food platforms. Review Ocasio's role and vendor licences before enabling food bookings. Additional local venue/fire, food, alcohol, sound, copyright/music, labour and insurance requirements depend on category and event location; they were not exhaustively established here and need a category-specific checklist.

[TRAI sender guidance](https://www.trai.gov.in/advice-to-senders) addresses principal-entity/header/template processes for bulk messaging. Channel onboarding and operational/promotional classification need review; booking consent is not unrestricted marketing permission.

For hosted reviews, portfolios or messaging, assess intermediary/content duties separately using the [MeitY rules collection](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/10/44e62510fb815a5cc72d094c7a5493b6.pdf), checking subsequent amendments and court orders. The linked compilation says updated 6 April 2023 despite its later upload path; it must not be represented as a fully consolidated November 2026 text. No safe-harbour conclusion or universal content-grievance deadline is asserted here.
